name: Financial assumptions
description: Keep break-even estimates explicit and distinguish missing inputs from zero.
---

Do not invent basket splits or operating costs for the business. Break-even requires a food/drink spending split and per-shift staffing plus allocated overhead; a gross-margin range alone cannot establish it.

**Why:** The deck's original guest counts and average checks do not establish those missing business assumptions. Supplying plausible-looking defaults would present unsupported numbers as facts.

**How to apply:** Leave unknown assumptions unset until entered, distinguish unset values from explicit zero, and identify the food/drink margins used. Staffing and overhead inputs must exclude ingredient costs already covered by gross margins.

## Financial presentation

Show one conservative **Profit Estimate**, never a low/high profit range. Display euro amounts in full with thousands separators, never shortened with `k` or `M`.

**Why:** The user repeatedly rejected ranges and abbreviated amounts such as “387.5k,” and explicitly said they had already requested their removal.

**How to apply:** Apply this to every slide, chart label, summary card, financial-model screen and export. Gross profit estimates remain before staffing, rent and other operating costs; do not relabel them as net profit.

## User-supplied planning baseline

Assume rent plus utilities of €3,000/month and 50 guests for each shift.

**Why:** The user supplied these figures for the planning scenario, not as verified lease or sales data.

The user approved applying the revised scenario: food/drinks spending of €4/€6 morning, €10/€5 lunch and €16/€6 dinner; staffing allowances of €144, €240 and €240 per shift, respectively; five operating days/week. They subsequently set food margin to 45% and coffee margin to 55%.

**How to apply:** These are approved planning estimates, not validated costs or observed sales. Keep rent/utilities separate from insurance, administration and other excluded costs. Changes to guest counts are shared across the deck's revenue forecasts; rent and staffing are used for the slide-five break-even estimate.

## Takeaway

Assume 20 takeaway orders per day, an €18 average ticket, and a 55% gross margin on both takeaway food and coffee.

**Why:** The user supplied these assumptions when adding a Takeaway row after Dinner on slide six.

**How to apply:** Treat takeaway as additional sales, separate from dine-in guests. Use its own margin when combining forecasts, rather than applying the dine-in margin to all revenue. Keep the three dine-in shifts distinct from this all-day sales channel.
